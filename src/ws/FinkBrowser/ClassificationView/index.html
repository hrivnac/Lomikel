How to read the map

Classification neighborhood

The red star is the selected alert and blue stars are its nearest JanusGraph neighbors. Edge labels report their original graph distance.

Classes are arranged around the outer ring using raw common-alert intersection counts. The force projection makes stronger overlaps tend to smaller angular separations, but it is not an exact distance scale.

Object positions are an approximate 2D projection: direction reflects classification affinity, while distance from the selected alert reflects JanusGraph distance. Coincident symbols are fanned apart slightly; when two alerts have identical affinity, their direction is mathematically undefined and deterministic spacing is used only for selection.

Alerts with an exact zero graph distance are separated by a tiny dashed visual offset so that each remains selectable; their displayed distance stays 0.

Hover, focus, or tap a star for classifications. Press Enter on a focused star, or double-click it, to make that alert the new center. Pan by dragging the map and zoom with the wheel or pinch gesture.

LSST and ZTF use their configured plaintext graph endpoints. ZTF availability depends on firewall or tunnel access; no unrelated demo data is substituted.
